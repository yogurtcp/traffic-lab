# Verification — 18 October 2026

## Automated model checks

`npm test`: 54 passing tests. Covers evenly spaced starts, seeded reproducibility, the leftmost slowpoke, lap geometry and speed units, recovery to all eight desired speeds without overshooting, smooth homogeneous flow across unequal lap lengths, reaction delay against an unbraked control, temporary braking versus persistent breakdown, repairs, obstacle rejection/removal, passing rules on both sides and across nonadjacent lanes, front/rear merge gaps, dense traffic with maximum reaction delay, queue separation, and a brake-induced traffic wave.

Nine CLI tests cover exact driver mixes, scenario/flag precedence, invalid settings and event times, deterministic fixed-step event execution and statistics, one-based obstacle lanes, breakdown/repair/removal, explicit reporting of rejected obstacle placements, early rejection of nonexistent car IDs, JSON-only runs, overwrite protection, help, and unknown flags. The no-video integration check completes two laps in 80 simulated seconds with one unimpeded Regular.

Twelve layout/junction tests cover config migration, continuous closed paths and smooth seams/corners, all four approaches, full-lap throughput (including repeated laps and exclusion of an initial partial marker crossing), safe spawn placement, road hit testing at 360 px and 800 px with 1/3/5 lanes, stop/dwell and arrival order, light phases, rolling roundabout entry, conflict-free reservations with maintained vehicle separation, and repair/release of an occupied junction. Mixed-personality traffic runs through every intersection mode with one and three lanes for 75 simulated seconds each. Queued lanes advance independently near junctions; this prevents an admitted car being trapped by the ordinary no-undertaking guard beside another stopped lane.

Seven merge regressions check that Regulars reject a physically clear gap when it would force sharp braking, reserve extra room for a longer reaction delay, wait for an approaching car to pass, and complete a well-spaced merge without forcing the follower above 1.5 m/s² braking during the first four seconds. New cases verify a smaller left-merge gap with 38 m behind, returning right with 30 m ahead and 38 m behind, and a crawling merge at 10 km/h with 5 m ahead and 8 m behind. The first two tighter moving merges also check actual follower braking stays within 2.5 m/s² over four seconds, and the right-merge case rejects a close 110 km/h follower. Every simulated step checks separation. The check includes the merging Regular's own slowing, the destination follower's comfort setting, and lane-length conversion; other ordinary personalities keep their stricter left-merge rules. Stuntman cut-ins and right-side obstacle bypasses retain their exceptions. Homogeneous flow and the 36-Regular obstacle-queue regression remain part of the suite.

The slowpoke passing regression was reproduced before the fix: a slowpoke doing 70 km/h chose to pass a leader also doing 70, and a driver chose a safe passing lane even when its traffic was slower. Three regression tests now cover refusing equal-speed/faster leaders (including a 60 km/h follower behind a 65 km/h leader), preserving useful passes after following a genuinely slower car, and rejecting a passing lane without a speed advantage. Passing decisions continue to use delayed traffic perception; obstacle avoidance remains exempt from the discretionary speed-gain requirement.

A separate incomplete-pass regression reproduced a Regular leaving the left lane before getting past a 70 km/h Slowpoke, braking behind it, and repeating the maneuver. Two new end-to-end tests cover 144 passes across circle/rectangle layouts, two/three lanes, 0/0.9/2-second delays, 60/65/70/90 km/h starting speeds, and 30/45/70 m initial gaps. They require a completed left-side pass, no return right before passing, acceleration above 85 km/h, and eventual return to the rightmost lane within 60 seconds, while checking physical separation every step. Additional three/five-lane cases exercise a car held back by the no-undertaking rule, including a nonadjacent slower lane: it moves left in stages, passes legally, and returns right. The fix preserves the smaller merge margins and follower-braking checks.

The left-lane obstacle deadlock was reproduced with 36 Regular drivers on both two- and three-lane roads: all vehicles eventually stopped because the no-undertaking guard also blocked traffic beside the obstruction queue. Four regression tests cover a direct right-side bypass, bypassing a connected queue, adjacent traffic clearing room for a blocked car to merge, preserving ordinary passing restrictions for an unrelated distant obstacle, and sustained useful flow after 90 simulated seconds on both road configurations. Queue membership is computed from the connected following chain behind a stationary obstruction, with gaps and speed limiting its extent; delayed snapshots drive lane choice while current membership drives the final guard.

Three Stuntman tests confirm acceptance of tight gaps rejected by Regular drivers, choosing a better lane even when both acceleration estimates saturate at hard braking, and continued rejection of physically insufficient clearance. A cut-in is compared with the same traffic without that maneuver: the following Regular driver brakes harder than 3 m/s² and loses over 2 m/s of speed relative to the control within 1.5 seconds. Every step in that comparison checks separation in all occupied lanes. Brake lights also illuminate when the collision guard intervenes.

Stress cases ran for 30 simulated seconds each on 1, 3, and 5 lanes with a short road, mixed traffic, two-second delay, and incidents. These checks confirm finite nonnegative speeds, wrapped positions, and at least 0.49 m clearance in every occupied lane. Additional 100-second preset runs exercised sustained flow and incident recovery. Perfect flow was checked for 60 seconds: no cars below 30 km/h and no lane changes.

## Interface checks

An independent JSDOM 26.1.0 harness loaded the real HTML and application modules, supplying a mocked Canvas context and deterministic animation callbacks. It exercised initialization of all eight driver rows, exact totals, the vehicle list, playback and stepping, selection, braking, breakdown/repair, clearing incidents, presets, restart, road-pointer obstacle placement/removal, the explanation dialog, reaction-time edits, and empty-road guidance. It also switches layouts, checks when the intersection selector is enabled, applies all three junction controls, and verifies the displayed lap total and rate after forty one-second steps with one unimpeded car. Canvas drawing functions executed without exceptions. This is a DOM behavior check; it does not render page layout.

The actual road renderer was additionally exercised using offscreen Skia Canvas through `@napi-rs/canvas`, now a development dependency for the video CLI. Twenty PNG previews cover circle, rounded rectangle, and all three four-arm controls at 800 px and 360 px widths with two and five lanes. The extended approaches, constant-radius outer bends, wider roads, enlarged fixed-size car sprites, stop signs, signal colors, roundabout island, lane markings, and vehicle positions were visually inspected. Car drawings no longer shrink when gaps tighten; overlap between enlarged drawings is allowed while physical separation remains enforced. This also exposed an existing partial circle cutout caused by a reverse arc ending at 7 radians; both ring arcs now use exactly `2π`. Background path samples are cached per layout/viewport to avoid recomputing them for every animation frame.

Video export was exercised with bundled FFmpeg and Liberation fonts. The included scenario renders 180 simulated seconds into a 1440 × 1080, 30 fps, 30-second H.264 MP4 with brake, breakdown, and repair events, plus a JSON report and final-frame PNG. Start, midpoint, and final frames were inspected, and all 900 video frames decoded without errors. Its final statistics, all 181 metric samples, and all three intervention results exactly match an independent JSON-only run of the same scenario. Font registration makes text independent of system font matching. The browser remains free of runtime package dependencies.

The local server returned HTTP 200 with correct content types for the entry page and JavaScript. JavaScript syntax checks and the static production build passed. Publication was verified separately through GitHub Pages build state and HTTP requests for the public entry page and versioned app modules.
