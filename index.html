INSIDE LOOP LAB

Why does traffic stop
for no apparent reason?

A driver brakes. The next driver notices a moment later and brakes a little harder. Repeat that down a line of cars and a slow wave can travel backward, even after the original driver has moved on.

Run your own experiment

  1. Choose lanes, driver counts, and the shared reaction time.
  2. Apply, then press play. Cars begin evenly spaced within each lane, at their personality’s desired speed.
  3. Brake a car for three seconds, cause a breakdown, or place an obstacle on an empty patch of road.
  4. Compare the graphs. Red diagonal bands in the road view chart reveal moving slowdowns.

A few ground rules

Traffic travels counterclockwise. Lane 1 is the inner, leftmost passing lane. Regular and Speedster drivers pass moving traffic at 70 km/h or below on the left; Stuntmen can pass either way. A stopped obstruction can be avoided on either side. All drivers share your reaction delay.

Stuntmen top out at 160 km/h. Vehicles occupy both lanes during a lane change. Outer laps are longer. Driver colors show personality; brake lights and the speed overlay show slowdowns.

A model, with limits

Car following uses an Intelligent Driver Model–style acceleration rule with delayed traffic perception and custom lane choices. It’s an educational experiment, not a calibrated prediction. A final safety guard prevents overlapping cars, even with extreme reaction delays; it can brake faster than a real driver. There are no crashes.
