INSIDE LOOP LAB

Why does traffic stop
for no apparent reason?

A driver brakes. The next driver notices a moment later and brakes a little harder. Repeat that down a line of cars and a slow wave can travel backward, even after the original driver has moved on.

Run your own experiment

  1. Choose a road layout, lanes, arrival interval, driver probabilities, and the shared reaction time. Probabilities must add up to 100%, or use Zero all to clear the road and stop arrivals.
  2. Settings reset the road as you edit. Press play to start; changes during playback keep the simulation running. Continuous-arrival roads start empty. Each arriving car gets a randomly chosen personality, enters when there is room, and leaves after its visible trip. Blocked entrances keep an upstream queue. Fixed-car experiments remain available on loop layouts.
  3. Brake a car for three seconds, cause a breakdown, or place an obstacle on an empty patch of road.
  4. Use −10s or the Replay timeline to watch the last three simulated minutes again. Back to live returns to your paused experiment. Compare the graphs. Red diagonal bands in the road view chart reveal moving slowdowns.

A few ground rules

Circle and rectangle traffic travels counterclockwise. Lane 1 is the leftmost passing lane. Regular and Speedster drivers pass moving traffic at 70 km/h or below on the left; Stuntmen can pass either way. A stopped obstruction and its connected queue can be avoided on either side. All drivers share your reaction delay.

Stuntmen top out at 160 km/h and squeeze into small gaps, forcing other drivers to brake. Vehicles occupy both lanes during a lane change. Driver colors show personality; brake lights and the speed overlay show slowdowns.

Roads, junctions, and throughput

The sideways U has an entrance at the upper left and an exit at the lower left. The + junction has four entrances, with cars travelling to the opposite exit. The arrival interval is the total flow, shared across all entrances. Lane counts at a junction are per direction.

The figure-eight is a four-arm crossing joined by curved outer loops. Choose an all-way stop, traffic lights, or a roundabout. All-way stops take turns in arrival order after a complete stop. Lights alternate east–west and north–south with amber and clearance time. At the roundabout, approaching cars yield to traffic already circulating. This simplified roundabout admits one circulating car at a time; green lights can release multiple cars on one axis.

All drivers obey junction controls and hold their lane near the crossing. Try the eight-car Junction lab preset to compare modes. Place obstacles on approaches or outer loops; a breakdown inside the junction blocks it until repaired.

In arrival mode, throughput counts cars that exit the visible road, once per car. The rate is cars per simulated minute, averaged since reset. Paused time does not count. In fixed-car mode, throughput still counts full laps, including repeat laps by the same car.

A model, with limits

Car following uses an Intelligent Driver Model–style acceleration rule with delayed traffic perception and custom lane choices. It’s an educational experiment, not a calibrated prediction. A final safety guard prevents overlapping cars, even with extreme reaction delays; it can brake faster than a real driver. There are no crashes.
